# The Money Model Offer Playbook

Distilled framework for designing offer sequences that maximize cash collected fast. Use this as the canonical reference for every offer type, the rules that govern them, and how they assemble into a complete money model.

## 1. Core definitions and the 30-day rule

**Money model** = a deliberate *sequence* of offers — what you offer, when you offer it, and how you offer it — designed to make as much money as fast as possible from each customer.

**The bar that defines a good money model:** make more *profit* from one customer than it costs to *get and service* that customer, within 30 days or less.

**The bar that defines a $100M money model:** make more profit from a *single* customer in 30 days than it costs to get and service *multiple* (ideally 2+) new customers. When you clear this bar, cash stops being the constraint on growth — you can recycle the same money to acquire the next customers indefinitely.

**Why 30 days specifically:** a business can get ~30 days of interest-free money via a credit card. Pay it off inside the cycle and it behaves like cash you can redeploy. So if CAC is recouped inside 30 days, you can fund the next customer with the same dollars on repeat.

**The trap a money model avoids:** spending more to acquire a customer than you make back quickly, forcing you to cut advertising, starve the business of cash, and wait months/years to recoup — most businesses die here. The fix is to collect enough up front (and through the sequence) to pay yourself back fast.

**Compounding lever:** if you double per-customer value, you can afford to acquire ~2x customers at ~2x speed → roughly 8x growth (2×2×2). Triple it → ~27x. Small sequence changes compound dramatically.

## 2. The three stages and four offer types

A money model evolves through three stages. Build them in order; each stage funds the next.

- **Stage 1 — Get cash:** Attraction offers turn strangers into customers and cover acquisition cost.
- **Stage 2 — Get more cash:** Upsell offers (when they say yes) and Downsell offers (when they say no) raise 30-day profit per customer.
- **Stage 3 — Get the most cash:** Continuity offers maximize lifetime spend and add recurring revenue.

Four offer types map onto these stages:
1. **Attraction** — get the customer (free / discounted / great-deal entry).
2. **Upsell** — whatever you offer next (more, better, or new).
3. **Downsell** — whatever you offer after a "no" (never the same thing cheaper).
4. **Continuity** — ongoing value for ongoing payments until cancellation.

Any single offer can stand alone in any order; the magic is sequencing them at the right time.

## 3. Attraction offers

Purpose: turn eyeballs into customers and pull cash forward. They work because everyone wants a great deal — strangers can't verify value but always understand price, so discounts (up to and including free) make anything compelling. "Free," "discount," and "$1" are interchangeable levers; the bigger the discount, the stronger the pull.

### 3a. Win Your Money Back
Structure: customer pays; if they hit a goal you set (and show them how to reach), they get the money back as cash or store credit. They can qualify via **results**, **actions**, or **both**.
- Works best for things people start and quit (fitness, learning a skill, starting a business, beauty, habit change) — the incentive sustains them through the early grind.
- Good criteria are: (1) easy to track, (2) genuinely likely to produce the result, (3) advertise your business (posts, tags, reviews, referrals).
- It makes money because many won't qualify, and qualifiers stay as customers *if you have something else for them to buy* — so always pair with an upsell to apply winnings to (see Rollover Upsell).
- "Make everyone a winner": mid-program, credit their deposit toward the next/long-term offer as if they've already won, lowering failure anxiety and extending retention.
- Only run it if your current refund rate is below ~5%. Expect ~10% to ask for money back.

### 3b. Giveaways (sweepstakes / scholarship / raffle)
Structure: advertise a chance to win a big prize in exchange for contact info (and qualifying actions). Pick one grand-prize winner publicly, then privately offer *everyone else* the same thing at a discount (the "promotional offer" / partial scholarship).
- Grand prize = the thing you want everyone to buy, with a stated monetary value to anchor price.
- Promotional offer = your core offer with a discount justified by the anchor. The bigger the (believable) anchor, the bigger the apparent discount.
- Collect eligibility info and qualifying actions (answer questions, attend a call, make a post) to both qualify and amplify reach.
- Add urgency: run 3–7 days; daily countdown + value/social proof. Expiration on claiming the promotional offer too.
- "If your giveaway flops, the prize wasn't grand enough." Give two grand prizes (referrer also wins) to multiply referrals.
- Heavily regulated — obey local law; someone must actually win.

### 3c. Decoy Offer
Structure: advertise a lesser/free/discounted version; when the lead engages, present *both* the stripped decoy and a far more valuable **premium** offer side by side, emphasizing the premium.
- Premium has more features, benefits, bonuses, guarantees; decoy strips those (especially guarantees). Maximize the contrast — the value of the premium comes from the gap.
- Either choice is a win: premium = great; decoy = you keep the customer and can upgrade them later.
- Advertise benefits/dream outcome, not features. Discount offers get fewer but higher-show-up leads than free; use discounts where no-shows are costly (doctors, lawyers, dentists).
- If possible present premium first; ask "Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?" to earn permission to lead with premium.

### 3d. Buy X, Get Y Free
Structure: when a customer buys something, they get other stuff free. The more (and higher-value) free stuff, the stronger.
- Reframe pricing: instead of "3 shirts at $10," sell "1 shirt for $30, get 2 free" — same money, more perceived value. Free beats an equivalent percentage discount.
- Give *more* free than paid (buy 1 get 2 > buy 2 get 1). Free items can differ from paid items; more cheap free things can beat fewer expensive ones.
- Permanently raise prices before adding the free component to protect margin. Don't sell what you can't deliver — you may collect a year's cash in a month; budget to service the whole term.
- Great for fast cash from existing customers (cap at ~10% of base). Keep upselling prepayers — they spend the most.

### 3e. Pay Less Now or Pay More Later
Structure: offer a choice — pay a discounted price now, or pay full price later (with a conditional satisfaction guarantee). Either way you get a customer and usually some profit, plus a card on file.
- Pay-later = delayed payment + conditional guarantee; they can cancel before the charge if they qualified (e.g., attended) but weren't satisfied.
- Pay-now = 20–50% discount plus bonuses; offer it *after* they accept pay-later.
- Promise a clear, measurable, yes/no result you can deliver inside the window. Keep cancellations <10%; if higher, you over-promised, the guarantee bar is too low, or price too high.
- For events/workshops, hint at the next offer early to pre-sell it.

## 4. Upsell offers

"Upsell" = whatever you offer next. Every solved problem reveals the next problem; the upsell solves it. Upsells often carry the majority of profit (the burger is break-even; fries + drink + size make the money). Your first offer need not be profitable — find your "do you want fries with that?" Upsells fail when mistimed, mismatched to the problem, or untrusted. Forms: more (quantity), better (quality), new/complementary (different).

### 4a. Classic Upsell
"You can't have X without Y." Solve the next problem the instant the customer becomes aware of it (car → insurance → gas → late checkout).
- Offer the more profitable upsell first.
- "You don't want anything else, do you?" converts a trained "no" into a yes.
- Give access ASAP — value felt now beats value promised later; get it into their hands before they say yes if possible.
- Use free bonuses to *create* the next problem your upsell solves.
- Bundle and name bundles (Fastest Results Bundle, Transformation Package) — one ask, many sales; peel features off bundles to downsell later.
- BAM-FAM: "Book A Meeting From A Meeting." End every interaction by scheduling the next, with a known reason. Upsell as many times as there are problems to solve.
- Magnetic middle: add a third price option to nudge buyers toward the target tier (movie-popcorn pricing). You can also charge for guarantees/warranties/insurance instead of giving them free.

### 4b. Menu Upsell
Combine up to four tactics, in order:
1. **Unsell** — tell them what they *don't* need (cross it out) to build trust and spotlight what they do need.
2. **Prescribe** — tell them what they *do* need, how it integrates with what they bought, and exactly how to use it (assume the purchase).
3. **A/B** — ask preference between two options ("chocolate or vanilla?") instead of yes/no. Anything can be A/B (quantity, start date, flavor, time slot). Add a nudge ("most people love X").
4. **Card on file** — "Want to use the card on file?" removes friction. Collect a card on first purchase by asking which card they'd like to use.
- The Economist play: to sell the bundle, add a decoy priced equal to the bundle so the bundle becomes the obvious choice.

### 4c. Anchor Upsell
Present your most expensive option *first* (5–10x core price) → expect the "gasp" → come to the rescue ("Do you care about [premium feature]?") → present the core offer, which now looks like a great deal → ask for payment.
- Anchored customers spend more than planned; a handful still buy the premium (outsized profit), so always have a premium ready.
- Core and premium share the same *primary* features; only *secondary* features differ. Don't treat the anchor as fake — actually sell it, or it won't anchor. "The only thing worse than a $1,000 offer to a $100 budget is a $100 offer to a $1,000 budget."

### 4d. Rollover Upsell
Credit some/all of a prior purchase toward your next (more expensive) offer.
- **Who:** re-engage lapsed customers (win-back), rescue your own upset customers (better than refund), steal competitors' upset customers (credit what they owe elsewhere), upsell current customers.
- **What:** more / better / new — just keep profit after the credit.
- **How:** apply full or partial credit, upfront or spread over time.
- Price the next offer ≥4x the credit so even a full credit is ≤25% off. Add urgency (one-time, take-it-now). Gift-card play: sell $200 cards for $20 (cap 2, usable only on others), then roll them into a ≥$1,000 offer.

## 5. Downsell offers

"Downsell" = whatever you offer after a "no." Turning nos into yeses raises total cash from the same leads. It's "100 ways to offer the same product," not 100 products. **Iron rule: never the same thing for less** (that's discounting and destroys trust). You may offer something *different* for less.

Rules: a no is for *this* offer, not all offers; downsells are trades (give something, get something); personalize, don't pressure (stay a helpful guide); don't drop price just to close (test prices deliberately instead, because customers talk).

### 5a. Payment Plan Downsell
Same product, same total price, spread over time. A gamble: wins when you get more customers who complete payments; loses when full-pay buyers take a plan then cancel early. Seven-step ladder (stop when they buy):
1. Reward pay-in-full (bake interest into the list price, then offer a discount for prepaying — same math, better feel + price anchor).
2. Third-party financing → credit card → layaway.
3. Half now, half on next payday (then "what's the most you can put down today?").
4. Temperature check: "1–10, how badly do you want this?" 8+ keep going; ≤7 switch to a feature downsell.
5. Split into three (on paydays or monthly).
6. Evenly spread over the service term.
7. Free trial (see Trial with Penalty).
- Align charges to paydays; rerun declined cards same day. Seesaw downselling ("giant or tiny payments?") for less-experienced sellers. Watch that pay-in-full % doesn't drop. Longer billing cadence → far lower churn (annual ~2%/mo vs monthly ~10.7%/mo).

### 5b. Trial with Penalty
Try free *if* they meet your terms; they pay only if they *don't*. Terms mirror your Win-Your-Money-Back criteria (the actions that make great customers), but use fee-avoidance to drive adherence.
- Five steps: offer the trial last → always get a card ("what card do you want to use?") → sell "staying and paying" if results happen → explain fees *after* getting the card ("we charge to keep you on track") → make check-ins (your upsell moments) required.
- After the trial: liked it → upsell more/longer; hated it → take the blame, fix it, offer a better-fit (often higher) option (~half convert); didn't use it → reach out, waive fee for a meeting, get them back on track. Use this for recurring products/services (Pay-Less-Now for one-time/physical).

### 5c. Feature Downsell
Lower the price by changing *what they get* — less quantity, lower quality, cheaper alternative, or cut features entirely. Take something away, lower the price, ask "How about now?"
- Remove features from *highest to lowest value*. Removing a loved feature (e.g., the guarantee) and dropping price only a little makes people re-buy the original — they see the value once it's gone.
- Name combinations (Whale Package … Minimum Package); "so, nothing more than the minimum, then?" Temperature-check after two downsells; alternate feature downsells with payment plans to become hard to refuse. Barter discounts for reviews/testimonials/referrals. Done-for-you → done-with-you → do-it-yourself as a downsell ladder. Downsell current customers off unused features before they cancel (second-highest LTV cohort).

## 6. Continuity offers

Ongoing value for ongoing payments until cancellation. They add per-customer profit and one last thing to sell, and stack recurring revenue. **Don't use continuity as a standalone attraction offer** — it collects too little up front and crashes 30-day profit. Instead, **make continuity last**: run attraction + upsell + downsell to get cash first, then offer continuity, then upsell a bulk/prepaid block at a discount that rolls into month-to-month.

### 6a. Continuity Bonus
Give an awesome bonus (worth more than the first payment) for signing up today; sell the *bonus*, not the membership.
- Bonuses can be: more of the same, complementary, an upgrade, or limited-availability. Make them things you already have/do (past content, onboarding) with believable anchor values.
- Anchor the bonus first → "want to know how to get it free?" → "become a member today." Stack multiple bonuses to lift conversion.
- Standalone-vs-continuity pricing dial (set standalone price as a multiple of monthly): 1.33x→~50% choose continuity; 1.66x→~60%; 2x→~70%; 2.33x→~80%; 2.66x→~90%. Offer bulk prepaid discounts to spike 30-day profit.

### 6b. Continuity Discount
Give free time (now, later, spread, or after first payments) if they commit today. ("Free year of trash pickup if you sign a 5-year contract.")
- Apply discount: **upfront** (push the term; needs enforceable contracts/collateral), **at end** (earn free time by paying on time), **spread** (e.g., $600 over 12 mo = $50/mo off), or **after first 1–2 payments** (collect some cash first — preferred).
- Money levers: **bill every 4 weeks** (13 cycles/yr = +8.3% revenue for free); add a **3% processing fee**; get a **second form of payment / ACH** to cut failed payments. Offer a **lifetime discount earned at your biggest churn month**.
- Cancellation: set the cancellation fee = the discount they received (returns them to month-to-month). Make canceling easy; run exit interviews (waive fee for one) — saves a third to half.

### 6c. Waved Fee
Offer a month-to-month plan with a big setup fee (typically 3–5x monthly), *or* waive the fee if they commit to a term (≥1 year). Cancel inside the term → they pay the fee.
- Fees get them to start *and* stick: once it costs more to quit than to stay, they stay. Justify the fee as real setup cost ("commit long-term and I cover it"). Bigger fee → more commitments; smaller fee → more upfront cash. Best for long, slow-to-work services (SEO, weight loss, investing). Fee drops once the commitment is fulfilled.

## 7. Assembling and sequencing a money model

Map offers onto the three stages and sequence by the customer's moment of need:
- **Stage 1:** one attraction offer (+ its own downsell if needed) to get the customer and cover cost.
- **Stage 2:** an upsell at the point of greatest need; a downsell ladder for the nos. Alternate payment-plan and feature downsells.
- **Stage 3:** continuity offered *after* the cash offers, then a bulk prepaid upsell that rolls into month-to-month.

Build rules:
- **One offer / one stage at a time.** Implementing a full model at once breaks the business operationally and financially. Make each stage reliable, then automatic, then advance. Measure in quarters.
- **Raise prices in stages.** Launch cheap to collect yeses and feedback; raise price until extra cash from yeses no longer beats the lost nos.
- **Simple scales, fancy fails.** Aim for 100 *ways to offer* one product, not 100 products (e.g., 1/2/3/4 sessions per week).
- **Affiliate products fill gaps** without delivery headaches.
- **Turn an attraction offer into continuity** via automatic renewal (e.g., buy-6-get-6 → month-to-month after month 12).
- Mix and match freely — upsell tactics can live inside attraction offers, continuity can attract, etc. There are no rules; start with the canonical use, then experiment.

Worked archetypes (from the book) for inspiration:
- **B2B/coaching:** Decoy (free DIY vs paid done-with-you) → Classic upsell to premium tier → payment-plan downsell ladder → menu close + feature downsell → continuity. (Scaled to multi-million/month.)
- **Local fitness:** Win-Your-Money-Back challenge → payment-plan / trial-with-penalty downsell → menu upsell (supplements) → feature downsell → rollover upsell + lifetime-discount continuity.
- **Newsletter (digital):** Pay-Less-Now/Pay-More-Later free trial that is simultaneously attraction + upsell + continuity with a lifetime-rate lock.
- **Physical product (pet food):** Buy-X-Get-Y-Free → classic upsell (toys/vitamins) → feature downsell → auto-renew continuity.

## 8. Governing principles and ethics

- Every business has a money model; the question is "how will I make this work for *my* business," not "this won't work for mine."
- Give refunds when asked; fix mistakes. Don't take "blood money."
- Hard selling is for weak products. Make the offer available at the moment of need; if they decline, move on — it's a numbers game.
- Obey the law (especially around "free" and giveaways); consult counsel. Be transparent; don't lie — reputation can't be bankrupted away.
- Deliver on what you sell, especially when you collect cash ahead of service.
- Never negotiate price for the *same* thing. Pay-less-now = payment plan; pay-less-overall = feature downsell.

## 9. Quick-reference pricing/data heuristics

- CAC payback target: ≤30 days. $100M bar: one customer's 30-day profit ≥ cost to get + service 2+ customers.
- Win-your-money-back: run only if refund rate <5%; expect ~10% claim rate.
- Pay-now discount: 20–50%. Cancellation tolerance: <10%.
- Rollover: price next offer ≥4x the credit (≤25% discount).
- Anchor: premium = 5–10x core; share primary features.
- Continuity standalone multiple → % choosing continuity: 1.33x≈50%, 1.66x≈60%, 2x≈70%, 2.33x≈80%, 2.66x≈90%.
- Bill every 4 weeks → +8.3% annual revenue; +3% processing fee; ACH to reduce failed charges.
- Billing cadence vs monthly churn: monthly ~10.7%, quarterly ~5%, annual ~2%.
- Giveaway window: 3–7 days. Decoy: free → more leads, lower show; discount → fewer leads, higher show.
