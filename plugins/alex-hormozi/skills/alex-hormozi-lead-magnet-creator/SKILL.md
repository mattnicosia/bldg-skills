# Lead Magnet Creator

A lead magnet is a **complete solution to a narrow problem** — usually free or low-cost — that is valuable enough to charge for, and that, once solved, **reveals the next problem your core offer solves**. (Salty pretzels at a bar make you thirsty; the bar sells drinks.) A person who pays with time now is far likelier to pay with money later, and a great free offer lowers your cost to acquire a customer because far more people engage with free than with the core offer. Grand-Slam-Offer rules apply to free stuff too: make it so good people feel stupid passing it up.

## How to respond

If you don't yet know the user's **core paid offer**, their **ideal customer**, and the **problem they ultimately solve**, ask for them briefly (one short batch) — the lead magnet must hand off to the core offer, so you need to know what it hands off to. If the conversation already implies them, proceed.

Then walk the **7 steps** and deliver a concrete, named lead magnet (plus 2–3 alternatives). Don't just describe the steps — produce the actual artifact.

### Step 1 — Pick the narrow problem (and check the hand-off)
Choose a problem that is **narrow and meaningful** to the ideal customer, such that solving it makes the **next** problem obvious — and that next problem is what the core offer charges to solve. Narrow beats broad ("what's my home worth?" not "sell my home"). State the problem and name the next problem the core offer will solve.

### Step 2 — Choose the solution type (pick one)
- **Reveal a problem (diagnosis):** an audit/test/assessment that shows a gap that worsens with time (speed test, posture analysis, termite inspection). Great when delay is costly.
- **Sample / trial:** brief but full access to the core offer. Great when the core offer is a recurring solution to a recurring problem (a free adjustment, a free week, a consumable sample — Costco).
- **One step of a multi-step process:** give one valuable step free, sell the rest (first coat of sealant; free finance calculator that reveals the work involved). Great for complex core offers.
- **Quiz as magnet (pattern):** a short personalization quiz that diagnoses segment/need and hands them into a tailored CTA (often paired with affiliate/influencer traffic on a low-margin front while backend monetizes). Use when personalization raises perceived likelihood and the next problem after the quiz result is your paid offer.

### Step 3 — Choose the delivery method (pick one)
**Software** (tool/calculator/spreadsheet), **information** (mini-course/guide/checklist/interview), **service** (you do work for free — audit, setup, first session), or **physical product** (something they hold). Note: you can usually make several versions of the same magnet across delivery types and rotate them to keep advertising fresh.

### Step 4 — Name it (test the headline first)
The headline/name does ~80% of the work — more people read it than anything else. Make it specific and benefit-led; tools/checklists read as higher value than "another training." Offer **2–3 name candidates** and, if possible, suggest the user test them (poll, post, or ask) before committing.

### Step 5 — Make it easy to consume
Lower the effort to say yes and to use it: multiple formats and devices for info; available at many times/ways for services; simple to order/open/use for products. Easy consumption can multiply take-and-use rates several-fold.

### Step 6 — Make it genuinely excellent
Give away the secret; sell the implementation. The free thing should provide more value than competitors' paid stuff — your reputation is built on it, and ~99% who consume it won't buy but will judge you by it. Don't give sucky fluff to "protect" the paid offer; that backfires.

### Step 7 — End with a clear CTA
Tell them exactly **what to do next** (one action — book, reply, click, download) **and a reason to act now**: scarcity (limited slots/quantity — honest, real caps), urgency (a real deadline), or even an honest made-up reason (any reason beats none). One CTA at a time.

## Output format

Deliver the recommended lead magnet on a short spec:

```
### [Lead magnet name]  (2-3 alternates: …)
- Narrow problem it solves: …
- Next problem it reveals → core offer: …
- Type: [reveal / sample / one-step]
- Delivery: [software / info / service / physical]
- Why it's easy to consume: …
- The CTA: "[what to do]" + [scarcity/urgency/reason to act now]
```

Then note the **one thing to test first** (usually the name) and a one-line reminder that the magnet only works if it's advertised — point to the lead-machine skills for distribution.

## Guardrails
- The magnet must hand off to the core offer; a freebie that doesn't reveal the next problem just attracts freebie-seekers.
- Keep it narrow — a broad "solve everything" freebie is weak and expensive to deliver.
- Honest scarcity/urgency only (real caps, real deadlines).
- If the underlying paid offer is weak, that's an offer problem, not a lead-magnet problem — point to the grand-slam-offer skills.
