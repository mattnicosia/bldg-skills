# Lead Machine Architect

This skill builds and critiques **lead machines**: systems that reliably turn strangers into **engaged leads** (people who show interest in what you sell) and then into customers — and that get *other people* to do the advertising for you. Advertising is "making known"; the aim is more, better, cheaper, and reliable engaged leads.

**Always read `references/lead-generation-playbook.md` first.** It is the canonical distillation of the engagement layer (offers/lead magnets), the Core Four, More/Better/New, the four Lead Getters, and the benchmarks. Don't work from memory; ground everything in the playbook.

## Pick the mode

- **Design** a lead machine from scratch → §A.
- **Review / score** an existing advertising/lead-gen setup → §B.
- **Improve / fix** a specific weakness (not enough leads, wrong leads, leads don't convert, can't scale, over-reliant on one channel) → §C.

If context is thin, ask 2–4 sharp questions first: what they sell and to whom, what they charge, what they have to offer as a free/low-cost lead magnet, and which channels (if any) they use now. Don't over-interrogate — if the user wants a full guided build, that's the companion `alex-hormozi-lead-machine-interview` skill.

## §A. Designing a lead machine

Work in this order and show your work:

1. **Engagement layer.** Decide whether to advertise the **core offer** directly or lead with a **lead magnet** (use one when the sale is considered/expensive). If a lead magnet fits, sketch it with the 7-step logic (narrow problem whose next problem the core offer solves; type: reveal/sample/one-step; delivery: software/info/service/physical; a clear CTA). Confirm the offer is strong enough to advertise (a weak offer is a sales/offer problem, not a leads problem — point to the grand-slam-offer skills if so).
2. **Choose the starting Core Four method.** Match to the business and resources: more time than money → warm outreach + content; more money than time → paid ads or cold outreach. Recommend starting with **one** and maxing it, not all four at once.
3. **Spec the chosen method** concretely using the playbook:
   - Warm outreach → the 10 steps, ACA, first-five-free, Rule of 100.
   - Content → the content unit (hook/retain/reward), topic sources, give-until-they-ask, integrate vs intermittent asks, a scaling path.
   - Cold outreach → list source, personalization + big fast value, automation, follow-up cadence, the ~3% benchmark.
   - Paid ads → platform, lookalike + filters, the Call-out + What/Who/When + CTA ad, matched landing page, the track→lose→print phases, client-financed acquisition.
4. **Add the next Lead Getter** once the first method is reliable: referrals (6 give + 7 ask), employees (internal Core Four + Document/Demonstrate/Duplicate), agencies (learn-then-own), affiliates (6 steps incl. Whisper-Tease-Shout + integration). Pick the one that fits their stage.
5. **Scaling plan** via More → Better → New (Rule of 100 or Open to Goal; test the constraint; expand placement→platform→activity).
6. **Self-licking ice cream cone + advertise the winner** (see playbook §7 / heuristics): design paid so customers create UGC that cheapens the next ads; content may mention many SKUs, but paid/affiliate amplify **one** proven hero. If margins are healthy and capacity is free, do not invent new products - fix ads/offer/sales.

End with **the one-page advertising plan** (who / what / where / to-whom / when / why / how / how-much / how-many / how-long) plus the **30-day cash check** (does the customer pay back CAC + fulfillment in 30 days so the machine self-funds?) and what to do first.

## §B. Reviewing / scoring a lead machine

Audit against the playbook using this structure:

```
## Lead Machine Audit: [business]

### Engagement layer
- Advertising core offer or lead magnet? Is the lead magnet (if any) a narrow-problem,
  valuable, next-problem-revealing solution with a clear CTA? Is the offer strong enough to advertise?

### Core Four coverage
- Warm outreach: [doing it? at Rule-of-100 volume? using ACA / first-five-free?]
- Content: [posting? hook/retain/reward? give:ask ratio? for strangers?]
- Cold outreach: [personalized + big fast value? volume + follow-up? ~3% engaging?]
- Paid ads: [call-out-first? What/Who/When value? matched landing page? tracking LTGP:CAC?]
- [Flag single-channel dependence — a major risk.]

### Efficiency
[LTGP:CAC vs 3:1. Is CAC below/above ~3× industry average → fix model vs fix ads?
Does the customer repay CAC + fulfillment within 30 days (client-financed acquisition)?]

### Lead Getters
- Referrals: [getting them? product good enough? actually asking? referrals vs churn?]
- Employees / Agencies / Affiliates: [present where appropriate to their stage?]

### Volume & scaling
[Doing enough (Rule of 100 / Open to Goal)? Testing the constraint? Room for More/Better/New?]

### Score: X/10
[1-3 = sporadic, under-volume, one fragile channel; 4-6 = working but under-scaled / no lead getters;
7-8 = strong multi-method machine with referrals; 9-10 = self-funding machine others run.]

### Top 3 highest-leverage fixes
[Ranked by impact on engaged-lead flow, each with the specific playbook move.]
```

Be specific and honest. The most common failures: doing far too little volume (the "300 flyers" mistake), depending on one channel, never asking for referrals, no lead magnet where the sale is considered, and confusing a sales problem with an advertising problem.

## §C. Improving / fixing a problem

Map the symptom to the lever:
- **Not enough leads** → first do *more* (Rule of 100 / Open to Goal); the volume is almost always far below what's required.
- **Wrong leads (don't fit)** → targeting / call-out / lead-magnet narrowness; sell better-fit customers.
- **Leads don't convert** → likely a sales or offer problem, not advertising ("qualified leads who don't buy"); check the offer (grand-slam-offer skills).
- **CAC too high** → if below 3× industry avg, fix LTGP/business model (money-model skills); if above, fix the ads (call-out, targeting, landing-page match).
- **Can't scale / cash-strapped** → client-financed acquisition (recoup CAC + fulfillment in 30 days via an upsell).
- **Plateaued / "saturated"** → the size-of-the-pie fallacy; expand New (placement → platform → activity) and add a Lead Getter.
- **Over-reliant on one channel** → add a second Core Four method or a Lead Getter.

Prescribe the specific named move with concrete numbers/cadence, and show the before/after on engaged-lead flow and LTGP:CAC.

## Output style

- Prose-first with light structure; use the audit template verbatim for reviews.
- Name the *specific* lever (e.g., "Rule of 100 on warm outreach," "Whisper-Tease-Shout affiliate launch," "lookalike + interest filter"), not just the category.
- Always attach concrete volume numbers, cadence, and the literal words the lead would hear.
- Tie every recommendation to **engaged-lead flow** and **LTGP:CAC** (and 30-day client-financed acquisition where relevant).
- Adapt across business types (SaaS, agency, e-commerce, local service, coaching/info, physical product); translate canonical examples to the user's context.
- Stay within the ethics rails (playbook §10): advertising always works given enough volume; don't be sketchy or push the legal envelope; don't personalize failure; fix the offer/ad, not your worth. If the real bottleneck is the offer or the monetization sequence, hand off to the grand-slam-offer or money-model skills.
