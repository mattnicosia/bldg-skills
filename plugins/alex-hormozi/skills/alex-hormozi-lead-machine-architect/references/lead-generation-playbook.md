# The Lead Generation Playbook

Canonical distillation of the method for getting strangers to want to buy your stuff — i.e. generating **engaged leads** at scale. Advertising is "the process of making known": you let people know about your stuff so more of them buy it. This playbook covers what a lead really is, how to get leads to engage, the only four ways to advertise (the Core Four), how to get other people to advertise for you (the four Lead Getters), and how to scale it all (More/Better/New).

Ground all lead-machine design, review, and improvement in this playbook rather than working from memory.

## 1. Definitions: leads vs engaged leads

- **Lead** = a person you can contact (a number, email, follower, address). Leads alone aren't enough.
- **Engaged lead** = a person who has *shown interest* in the stuff you sell. Engaged leads are the true output of advertising and the thing this whole playbook optimizes.
- The goal: **more, better, cheaper, and reliably-from-many-places** engaged leads. All else equal, double your leads → double your business.
- Two growth levers only: get more customers, and make each worth more. This playbook is the "more customers" lever.

---

## 2. The engagement layer: offers and lead magnets

To turn a lead into an engaged lead you advertise one of two things:
- **Core offer** — the main thing you sell. Advertise it directly (the direct path to money). Try this first; it may be all you need.
- **Lead magnet** — a complete solution to a *narrow* problem, usually free or low-cost, that (a) is valuable enough to charge for, and (b) once solved, reveals the next problem your core offer solves. (Salty-pretzels-at-a-bar logic.) A person who pays with time now is likelier to pay with money later. Grand-Slam-Offer principles apply to free stuff too — make the lead magnet so good people feel stupid saying no.

**7 steps to a great lead magnet** (also the standalone lead-magnet-creator skill): (1) pick a narrow problem for a specific customer such that your core offer solves the *next* problem; (2) decide the solution type — **reveal a problem** (diagnosis), a **sample/trial**, or **one step of a multi-step process**; (3) choose delivery — **software**, **information**, **service**, or **physical product**; (4) test the name/headline (headline first — 80% of the effect); (5) make it easy to consume (every format/device); (6) make it genuinely excellent (give away the secret, sell the implementation); (7) end with a clear **CTA**: what to do + a reason to act now (scarcity, urgency, or even a made-up-but-honest reason).

A good lead magnet even with a delivery cost *lowers* CAC, because far more people engage with free than with the core offer.

## 3. The Core Four (the only four ways to advertise)

Two audiences × two communication modes = the only four things a single person can do to let others know about their stuff:

| | One-to-one (private) | One-to-many (public) |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm** (know you) | Warm outreach | Post free content |
| **Cold** (strangers) | Cold outreach | Paid ads |

You trade time, money, or both. You only need to master **one** to feed yourself — but they compound (content makes outreach and ads convert better, etc.). General sequencing: start with warm outreach → add content → add cold outreach and/or paid ads. If you have more time than money, lean content/outreach; more money than time, lean ads/cold.

---

## 4. Core Four #1 — Warm outreach (1-to-1, warm)

Reach out personally to people who already know you. Cheapest, most reliable, most neglected. Everyone has a list (phone contacts, email, social followers).

**10 steps:** (1) compile your list across all platforms; (2) pick the platform with the most contacts; (3) personalize the opener with something real about them; (4) reach out to **100/day**, up to 3 touches each; (5) when they reply, use **ACA** — **A**cknowledge, **C**ompliment, **A**sk another question — to steer toward your topic; (6) make an offer framed via the **Value Equation** (dream outcome × perceived likelihood ÷ time delay ÷ effort & sacrifice), often phrased as "do you know anyone who…" so it isn't pushy; (7) make it easy to say yes — start with the **first five free** (in exchange for use, feedback, and a testimonial); (8) work the whole list, then start over on the next platform; (9) **start charging** once referrals appear — raise price ~20% every 5 sales; (10) keep the list warm with ongoing value (Dean Jackson's "Are you still looking to ___?" nine-word re-engagement).

**Benchmark:** ~1 in 5 contacts engage; ~1 in 5 of those take the free offer; ~1 of those converts to paid later → roughly **1 customer per 100 reachouts**. 500 reachouts/week at a $1,400 offer ≈ $100k/yr from warm outreach alone.

## 5. Core Four #2 — Post free content (1-to-many, warm)

Say it once, reach many. The compounding asset is the **audience**, not the content. Content grows the warm audience; the warm audience feeds warm outreach and makes all other advertising convert better (e.g., the case where 78% of buyers had consumed 2+ pieces of content first).

**The content unit = Hook → Retain → Reward** (the smallest unit that does all three; link units together for long-form):
- **Hook (get attention):** pick a **topic** (far past / recent past / present / trending / manufactured), write a **headline** using ≥2 news drivers (recency, relevancy, celebrity, proximity, conflict, unusual, ongoing), and match the **format** of what already wins on the platform. Spend most effort on the first 5 seconds.
- **Retain (hold attention):** drive curiosity by embedding open questions via **lists, steps, and stories** (interweave freely). There's no too-long, only too-boring.
- **Reward (satisfy attention):** value per second; deliver on the hook's promise. If your audience grows, your content is good enough; if not, it isn't.

**Monetize via the give-ask ratio:** growing platforms over-give and under-ask, so **give, give, give… until they ask** (give in public, ask in private). When you do ask, either **integrate** (keep give:ask ≥3:1 inside content) or run **intermittent** ask-posts; each ask advertises the core offer or the lead magnet, then return to value.

**Scaling:** depth-then-width (max one platform, then add) or width-then-depth (be everywhere, then maximize). Make content **for strangers**. One CTA at a time. Volume works: 10× the content → ~10× audience growth.

## 6. Core Four #3 — Cold outreach (1-to-1, cold)

The advanced cousin of warm outreach — same mechanics, no warm-audience limit. Strangers create three problems, solved in order: (1) get a way to contact them (build/buy/scrape lists); (2) get attention; (3) get interest.

Two levers to beat the trust gap: **personalize** (make the cold message look warm — 1–3 real facts + a compliment; rewriting below a 3rd-grade reading level lifted replies ~50%) and **big fast value** (give away something people *actually pay for* — swapping a weak lead magnet for a genuinely generous one 3×'d take rates). Then **volume**: automate delivery (pre-recorded/templated), automate distribution (blasts/dialers where ethical), and **follow up more times in more ways** (then recycle the list every 3–6 months as circumstances change).

**Benchmarks:** email ~30% open × ~10% reply → ~3% engage; aim for ≥**3:1 LTGP:CAC**. Count in 100s; don't set a daily goal below 100 or stop before 100 days. Cold outreach is private (competitors can't see it), reliable (inputs→outputs), platform-change-resistant, and hard to copy — but labor-intensive and slow to scale (often ~a year). For low-ticket products, point cold outreach at **affiliates** rather than end customers.

## 7. Core Four #4 — Paid ads (1-to-many, cold)

Rent eyeballs. Fastest way to reach the most people; a game of **efficiency**, not reach. Narrow from "the whole world" to the right people:

1. **Pick a platform** you understand, can target on, can format for, and can afford.
2. **Target:** build a **lookalike** from your best customer lists, then layer filters (age, geo, interest, etc.). Local markets are already narrow — don't over-filter.
3. **The ad = Call-out + Value + CTA.**
   - **Call-out** (most important; the first 5 seconds): verbal (labels, yes-questions, if-then statements, ridiculous results) and non-verbal (contrast, likeness, the scene). "Infinite ads": record ~30 first-lines × ~10 bodies → 300 variations.
   - **Value** via the **What/Who/When** framework: *What* = the 8 value elements (4 drivers + their 4 opposites/nightmares); *Who* = how the buyer's status changes in the eyes of others; *When* = past/present/future timeline. Toward good stuff, away from bad stuff.
   - **CTA:** one clear next action; reinforce with urgency/scarcity/bonuses. Match the **landing page** to the ad (continuous click-to-close experience).

**Money (three phases): track money → lose money → print money.** Set up tracking first. Expect to lose on most ads; budget up to ~2× the 30-day cash from a customer to test a new ad, kill it at ~1× if it gets no leads. When an ad wins, scale hard (reverse the budget from your customer goal, pad ~20%). **Efficiency metric = LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1.** If CAC is below ~3× industry average, fix the **business model (LTGP)**, not the ads; if above, fix the **ads**.

**Client-financed acquisition:** if the customer pays you more than it costs to **get + fulfill** them within **30 days** (via the first purchase plus an upsell), you recoup CAC immediately and recycle the cash forever — limitless scale. (This is the same 30-day logic as the money-model playbook.)

Don't confuse a **sales** problem (qualified leads who don't buy) with an **advertising** problem (wrong leads, or not enough).

**Self-licking ice cream cone (paid → UGC → cheaper ads).** Ads create customers; customers create UGC/proof; that proof makes the next ads cheaper and better. Design paid acquisition so it manufactures creative fuel, not just leads. If your ads never produce customer content you can reuse, you are buying traffic without building the flywheel.

**Advertise the winner (organic / multi-SKU trap).** Organic content creates optionality addiction: many products, many mentions, no forced pick. Content can *mention* many SKUs; paid amplify and affiliate point at **one** proven hero. Ship many; advertise one.

## 8. More / Better / New (scaling the Core Four)

When you want more engaged leads on your own, do the same activity **more**, **better**, or somewhere **new** — in that order under ~$1M/yr; flip to **better → more → new** above it.

- **More — the Rule of 100:** 100 primary actions/day for 100 days straight (100 reachouts, 100 min of content, 100 cold touches, or 100 min building paid ads). The bigger lever is usually doing more. **Open to goal:** commit to the *outcome* (e.g., 5 sales) no matter how long it takes, not just the action count. Facebook's top 0.1% test ~11× more creative — you can't scale a mediocre ad, only find exceptional ones by making far more.
- **Better — test against the constraint:** find the step with the biggest drop-off (usually near the front), test **one thing per week per platform**, log results, keep winners. Small constraint wins beat big non-constraint wins.
- **New — the size-of-the-pie fallacy:** markets are far bigger than they look. When more/better returns dip below what a new placement/platform/activity would give, expand in this order: **new placement → new platform → new Core Four activity.**

## 9. The four Lead Getters (get others to advertise for you)

Leverage = more leads per unit of your time. Get other people to do the Core Four for you. They start as leads, become engaged, then become lead-getters. Four types:

**9a. Customers → Referrals** (lowest cost, exponential). Referrals beat churn → organic compounding growth (Dropbox 39×'d in 15 months; PayPal). Two reasons businesses don't get them: the product isn't good enough, and they don't ask.
- **Build goodwill (6 ways, mapped to the offer parts):** sell better-fit customers (call-outs); set+beat expectations (lower promises until close rate dips); get more people better results (find what your best customers did, make everyone do it, and make it the guarantee condition); deliver faster wins (smaller increments, frequent updates, win in the first 48 hrs, BAM-FAM, deliver early); keep improving the product (monthly survey→fix→ship loop); tell them what to buy next.
- **Ask (7 ways):** one-sided benefit (pay your CAC to referrer or friend); two-sided (split CAC both ways — Dropbox/PayPal); ask at point of sale ("who else would get results doing this with you?"); referral as a negotiation chip; referral events (1–4 weeks); ongoing programs; unlockable bonuses. The "one question to rule them all": if you lost all customers but one, how would you treat them so they referred everyone they know?

**9b. Employees** (run your business and get leads on your behalf — the most valuable lead-getter, and what makes a business *sellable*). Get them via the **internal Core Four** (warm = ask network, cold = recruit, content = job posts, ads = promoted postings, plus referrals/agencies). Train with **Document → Demonstrate → Duplicate** (write the checklist, do it in front of them, have them do it). Reward following directions; if they follow the checklist and get the wrong result, the checklist is wrong. Hire experience for complex/operational roles; for entry-level lead work, train anyone with grit and give short windows to prove out.

**9c. Agencies** (rent skills, then own them). Don't fall for the "you'll never have to learn this" trap. Use agencies to **learn a new method or platform fast**, on an explicit deal: "work with us ~6 months, teach us why you make each decision, then we move to low-cost consulting once our team beats you." Run both teams until yours wins, then cut to consulting. Pick agencies by: word-of-mouth reputation, recognizable clients, a waitlist, realistic expectations, long-term (not hacky) strategy, clear asks/cadence/tracking, a strong offer, and — they're expensive (all good ones are; not all expensive ones are good).

**9d. Affiliates** (independent businesses that sell your stuff to *their* audience — highest leverage; can run on their own). **6 steps:** (1) find ideal affiliates ("who's got my leads?" — products/services/communities around your best customers); (2) make them a money-making offer (more money from current customers, high likelihood, no build/support, fast); (3) qualify them — get them invested (make them a customer and/or a paid-certified expert; charge ~10–20% of what an active affiliate earns in 12 months); (4) decide what/how to pay (pay for new + repeat customers; set payout from max-allowable CAC; use a **3-tier payout** — sign-up / activation / sustained performance — so blended payout < max CAC; pay with product where possible — "sell 3, get it free"); (5) **get them advertising via launches** — **Whisper → Tease → Shout** (curiosity → value elements → CTA, like movie trailers); (6) **keep them advertising via integration** — affiliate gives away your lead magnet with their offer, or sells your lead magnet, or sells your core offer directly. Employers of your consumers make great underused affiliates.

**Returns:** for affiliates, compare cost-to-get-an-affiliate against the gross profit of all customers they bring (target ≥3:1, ideally far higher).

## 10. Roadmap, ethics, and key beliefs

**Seven levels:** (1) friends know your stuff (warm outreach); (2) you consistently advertise at full personal capacity (warm outreach + content); (3) employees help you advertise; (4) product good enough for consistent referrals (≥25%); (5) advertise in more places/ways with a bigger team (≥2 methods, multiple platforms); (6) hire experienced executives to own channels; (7) the $100M machine — all cylinders firing without you. Realistic timeline: 5–10 years.

**One-page advertising plan:** pick the lead type (customers/affiliates/employees/agencies) → pick Rule of 100 or Open to Goal → fill the checklist (who/what/where/to-whom/when/why/how/how-much/how-many/how-long) → do it daily until you can pay someone else → then make *employees* the new target and repeat.

**Ethics & beliefs:** advertising **always works** — it's only a question of efficiency, so once you start, keep going until it works. Volume negates luck. The **many-sided die**: every die with a green side hits green if rolled enough; you don't know which roll others are on; you can't lose if you don't quit. Don't be sketchy or push the legal envelope — it follows you. Stop personalizing failure: if it doesn't work, the *offer/ad* needs work, not your worth.

## 11. Quick-reference heuristics

- Engaged lead, not just lead, is the target. More/better/cheaper/reliable.
- LTGP:CAC ≥ **3:1** to scale; below 3× industry-avg CAC → fix the model (LTGP), above → fix the ads.
- **Client-financed acquisition:** recoup CAC + fulfillment within **30 days** for limitless scale.
- Warm outreach ≈ **1 customer / 100 reachouts**; do 100/day.
- Cold outreach ≈ **~3% engage**; count in 100s, run ≥100 days.
- Content: **give until they ask**; give public, ask private; keep give:ask ≥ ~3:1.
- Paid ads: most effort on the **first 5 seconds / call-out**; test ~11× more creative; track → lose → print.
- Scaling order: **More → Better → New** (under ~$1M/yr); **Better → More → New** (above). Test one thing/week/platform against the **constraint** (biggest drop-off).
- Referrals **>** churn = organic compounding growth.
- Affiliates: 3-tier payout keeps blended payout < max CAC; activate via Whisper-Tease-Shout launches; retain via integration.
- Relationship to the other playbooks: the **lead magnet/core offer** is the Grand-Slam-Offer ("what you sell"); **client-financed acquisition** is the 30-day money-model logic ("how you sequence to recoup CAC"). Build the offer first, then advertise it, then sequence it.
- **Self-licking ice cream cone:** ads → customers → UGC/proof → cheaper/better ads. Build the loop on purpose.
- **Advertise the winner:** organic can show range; paid/affiliate amplify one hero SKU.
- **Constraint tree (margins + capacity):** if margins are healthy and you have free capacity, do **not** invent new products. Fix ads, offer, or sales. New SKUs are usually a distraction when the constraint is marketing.
