# Grand Slam Offer Architect

This skill builds and critiques **Grand Slam Offers**: single offers made so compelling and incomparable that the buyer's choice becomes "this vs. nothing." The aim is to widen the gap between value delivered and price charged so dramatically that you can charge a premium and still be a steal — turning a price-driven purchase into a value-driven one.

**Always read `references/grand-slam-offer-playbook.md` first.** It is the canonical distillation of the whole method — market selection, the Value Equation, the 5-step offer build, the enhancers (scarcity/urgency/bonuses/guarantees), and the MAGIC naming formula. Don't work from memory; ground everything in the playbook.

## Pick the mode

- **Design** a Grand Slam Offer from scratch → §A.
- **Review / score** an existing offer → §B.
- **Improve / fix** a specific weakness (low conversion, no differentiation, price resistance, refunds) → §C.

If business context is thin, ask 2–4 sharp questions first: what they sell, who the customer is and their painful problem, current price, and what outcome they promise. Don't over-interrogate — if the user wants a full guided build, that's the companion `alex-hormozi-grand-slam-offer-interview` skill.

## §A. Designing a Grand Slam Offer

Work the method in order; show your work at each step.

1. **Market check (gate).** Confirm the market is at least *normal* (growing, affordable, targetable) and the pain is real. If the market is shrinking or can't pay, say so plainly — no offer fixes a bad market. Note the niche; push for specificity (specificity multiplies price).
2. **Dream outcome.** State the destination/experience you're selling, framed around status and how others will perceive the buyer — not the vehicle.
3. **Problem list.** Walk the customer's sequence (before → during → after) and enumerate problems across all four value drivers (worth-it / will-it-work-for-me / too-hard / too-slow). Aim for many. Use the playbook **decommoditize checklist** (before / during / after service + proof layers) and note aspirational brand as a moat, not only bonuses/guarantees.
4. **Solutions list.** Reverse each problem into "how to [outcome] without [pain]." Cover *every* problem.
5. **Delivery vehicles.** Use the Delivery Cube (1-on-1/group/one-to-many; DIY/DWY/DFY; medium; responsiveness; the 10x/¹⁄₁₀th test) to generate options.
6. **Trim & stack.** Cut high-cost/low-value, then low-cost/low-value; keep low-cost/high-value and high-cost/high-value; favor high-value one-to-many assets. Present the survivors as a **named, value-stacked bundle**, each item with a benefit name and an ascribed dollar value, summing to a total that dwarfs the price.
7. **Price.** Set a premium price using the value-to-price gap (target a steep value:price ratio, e.g., stack value ≫ price). Justify via the virtuous cycle of price; make it sting a little.
8. **Enhancers.** Add at least one form each of **scarcity** (cap type) and **urgency** (cohort/seasonal/pricing/exploding), a **bonus stack** (using the bonus checklist, total bonus value eclipsing the core), and a **named guarantee** (pick the family that fits the margin/ticket; reverse the biggest fear; run the refund math).
9. **Name it.** Produce 3–5 candidate names via the MAGIC formula.

End with **the offer on one page** (the headline name, the dream-outcome promise, the value-stacked bundle with values + total, the price, the guarantee, scarcity/urgency terms) and a short note on what to test first.

## §B. Reviewing / scoring an offer

Audit against the playbook and produce a scorecard using this structure:

```
## Grand Slam Offer Audit: [offer / business]

### Market
[Normal/growing/shrinking? affordable? targetable? pain real? niche specific enough?]

### Value Equation
- Dream outcome (↑): [strong/weak — is it status-framed? selling destination not vehicle?]
- Perceived likelihood (↑): [proof, track record, specificity, guarantee present?]
- Time delay (↓): [fast early wins? speed emphasized?]
- Effort & sacrifice (↓): [DFY vs DIY? friction removed? psychological solutions used?]

### Differentiation & price
[Commodity or category-of-one? Competing on price or value? Premium or racing to the bottom?
Is value stacked with ascribed values, or one undifferentiated lump?]

### Enhancers
- Scarcity: [present? honest? a real cap?]
- Urgency: [present? a real deadline?]
- Bonuses: [stacked? named? values ascribed? do they eclipse the core? do they crush objections?]
- Guarantee: [present? conditional teeth? right family for the ticket/margin? fear reversed?]

### Naming
[Does it use MAGIC components? Is it punchy and avatar-specific, or generic/lazy?]

### Score: X/10
[1-3 = commodity, competes on price; 4-6 = some value but comparable/under-enhanced;
7-8 = strong, differentiated; 9-10 = true category-of-one Grand Slam Offer.]

### Top 3 highest-leverage fixes
[Ranked by likely impact on response × conversion × price, each with the specific
playbook move to apply.]
```

Be specific and honest. The most common failures: selling the vehicle instead of the dream outcome, an undifferentiated value lump (no stack/ascribed values), no real guarantee, and lazy/generic naming.

## §C. Improving / fixing a problem

Map the symptom to the lever:
- **Low ad response** → wrapper/naming (MAGIC) and the magnet/reason-why; check the dream outcome framing.
- **Low close rate** → value stack, bonuses that crush specific objections, and the guarantee.
- **Price resistance / commoditized** → widen the value:price gap (add value, don't cut price), premium reframe, anchor with bonuses, niche down for specificity.
- **High refunds** → tie guarantee conditions to success actions; consider conditional/performance families; check product quality.
- **Stalling / no urgency** → add a real deadline (cohort/seasonal/pricing) and scarcity cap.
- **Fatiguing offer** → walk the fatigue rotation order (creative → copy → headline → duration → enhancer → monetization).

Prescribe the specific named move with concrete wording and numbers, and show the before/after on the value equation and the value:price gap.

## Output style

- Prose-first with light structure; use the audit template verbatim for reviews.
- Name the *specific* lever (e.g., "conditional service guarantee," "growth-rate-cap scarcity," "MAGIC name"), not just the category.
- Always ascribe concrete dollar values in stacks and give the literal words the customer/prospect would hear.
- Tie every recommendation back to the three multipliers — **response × conversion × price** — and the value-to-price gap.
- Adapt across business types (SaaS, agency, e-commerce, local service, coaching/info, physical product); translate canonical examples to the user's context explicitly.
- Stay within the ethics rails (playbook §12): truthful claims, honest scarcity/urgency, solve every problem, a guarantee can't rescue a bad product.
