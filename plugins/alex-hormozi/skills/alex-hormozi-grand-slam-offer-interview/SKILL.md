# Grand Slam Offer Interview

This skill interviews the user about their business, then delivers **one complete, ready-to-sell Grand Slam Offer** (plus a leaner alternative and naming options). The book's method is to build *one* great offer and enhance it — so the deliverable is a single fully-built offer, not several half-built ones.

**Read `references/grand-slam-offer-playbook.md` before and during the process.** It is the canonical source for the Value Equation, the 5-step build, the enhancers, and the MAGIC naming formula. Ground the interview and the final offer in it.

## How the interview works

Ask **12–18 questions** in **small batches (3–5 at a time)**, plain language, adapting follow-ups to answers. Acknowledge what you learn between batches. Skip anything already answered earlier in the conversation. If an interactive question/options tool is available, use it for the multiple-choice items (easier on mobile); ask open-ended items as prose.

### Question bank (cover these; starred are essential; pick/adapt 12–18)

**Market & customer**
1. ★ What do you sell, in a sentence or two?
2. ★ Who exactly is the ideal customer? (push for a specific niche)
3. ★ What painful problem do you solve for them — and how badly does it hurt?
4. Can they afford a premium price, and are they easy to reach as a group (lists, communities, channels)?
5. Is the market growing, flat, or shrinking? (gate — a shrinking market changes everything)

**Dream outcome**
6. ★ What's the dream outcome — the destination/experience they really want? (not your product, their result)
7. How would hitting that change how others see them (status)?

**Problems & solutions**
8. ★ Walk me through what the customer has to do before, during, and after using what you sell — where do they get stuck, confused, or quit?
9. What's the #1 "but what about…?" objection that kills your sales?

**What you can deliver**
10. ★ What can you provide to solve those problems — list everything (calls, templates, tools, community, done-for-you work, check-ins, guarantees, anything)?
11. Which of those are cheap for you to deliver but high-value to them (especially anything you build once and reuse — guides, software, recordings)?
12. Could any partners/other businesses contribute bonuses (and would they pay you a referral commission)?

**Pricing & proof**
13. ★ What do you charge now, and what does it cost you to deliver one customer?
14. What proof do you have it works (results, track record, testimonials)? This sets the guarantee strength.
15. What result can you reliably produce, and how fast can you create an early win?

**Risk & constraints**
16. ★ What are customers most afraid of when they pay you? (this becomes the guarantee)
17. What's your delivery capacity (how many customers can you take per week/cohort)? (this becomes scarcity)
18. Any legal/claims sensitivities (health, income, "free," guarantees)?

Fold in the starred essentials, choose a set that fits, and stop once you can build responsibly (at least 12 unless the user cuts it short).

## Delivering the offer

Restate their situation in 2–3 sentences, then deliver:

**1) Market verdict (one line).** Normal/growing/shrinking, affordable, targetable — and whether to proceed or reposition first.

**2) The Grand Slam Offer — on one page:**

```
### [MAGIC name]
Promise (dream outcome): "[status-framed destination, with interval if allowed]"

The stack:
- [Named component 1 — benefit] .......... $[ascribed value]   (solves: [problem])
- [Named component 2 — benefit] .......... $[ascribed value]   (solves: [problem])
- [Named component 3 …] .................. $[ascribed value]
- Bonus: [named bonus] ................... $[value]            (crushes: [objection])
- Bonus: [named bonus] ................... $[value]
Total value: $[sum]
Your price: $[premium price]   (why it's still a steal: [value:price gap])

Guarantee: [named guarantee — the family/wording that fits their margin & fear]
Scarcity: [real cap from their capacity]
Urgency: [real deadline — cohort/seasonal/pricing]
```

Briefly show the **Value Equation** logic behind it: how the offer raises dream outcome + perceived likelihood and lowers time delay + effort.

**3) A leaner alternative.** A stripped, lower-ticket version (fewer components / more DIY) for a different segment or as a fallback — so there's a choice without building a whole second offer.

**4) Three candidate names** via MAGIC, noting which to test first.

**5) First moves.** What to launch/test first, and a reminder to create flow and monetize before adding friction — overdeliver early, then systematize.

## Style & guardrails

- Conversational during the interview; concrete and numeric in the offer (real ascribed values, real price, literal customer-facing wording).
- Name specific levers (the guarantee family, the scarcity cap type, the MAGIC name) — not vague categories.
- Sell the dream outcome, not the vehicle; make sure the stack solves every problem they surfaced.
- Respect ethics (playbook §12): truthful claims, honest scarcity/urgency, a guarantee can't fix a bad product; steer around any claims sensitivities they flagged.
- If the user wants to skip the interview, fall back to `alex-hormozi-grand-slam-offer-architect` design mode. If they then ask how to *sequence* this offer with upsells/downsells/continuity over time, point to the money-model skills.
