## 1. Discovery Questions

Ask conversationally — not as a survey. Let answers flow naturally.

### Opening (Broad → Narrow)
1. "Tell me about the business — what do you do and who do you do it for?"
2. "How long have you been operating?"
3. "What made you start this business?"

### Differentiation
4. "What makes you different from competitors?"
5. "What do you do that others refuse to do — or can't do?"
6. "What do you absolutely NOT want to be confused with?"
7. "If a competitor copied everything, what's the one thing they couldn't replicate?"

### Customer Understanding
8. "Who are your best customers? Describe 2-3 types."
9. "What emotional state are customers in when they first reach out?"
10. "What transformation do customers experience working with you?"
11. "What do customers say about you that makes you proudest?"
12. "What objections do you hear most often? How do you overcome them?"

### Aspirational Positioning
13. "What brands — in any industry — do you admire? Why?"
14. "If your brand were a person at a dinner party, how would they dress? Talk?"
15. "What's the one word you want people to associate with your brand?"
16. "What feeling should customers have after every interaction?"

### Voice & Tone
17. "Are there words or phrases you love using?"
18. "Words or phrases you hate — that competitors use?"
19. "More formal or casual? More expert or friendly?"
20. "Should your brand feel accessible or exclusive? Or between?"

### Practical Constraints
21. "Any legal, regulatory, or industry terms you must use or avoid?"
22. "Any claims you can or cannot make?"
23. "What channels do you communicate through most?"

### Avatar Deep-Dive (per customer type)
24. "What does this customer care about most?"
25. "What keeps them up at night?"
26. "What would make them say 'finally, someone who gets it'?"
27. "Where do they get information? Who do they trust?"
28. "What language do they use to describe their problem?"

## 2. Completed Example: ShadeScapes Americas

Use as a model for tone, structure, and depth of a finished brand book.

### Mission Statement
> "ShadeScapes curates refined shade and seating that blends beauty, performance, and longevity—enhancing how we live, gather, and experience outdoor spaces."

**Why it works:** Action verb "curates" (positions as selector, not seller). Benefits: beauty, performance, longevity. Outcome: enhancing how we live.

### Brand Promise
> ShadeScapes promises more than product — we promise partnership. From the first conversation, clients are guided by shade experts who listen, educate, and curate solutions from the world's finest European-influenced designers. We promise craftsmanship that endures, beauty that elevates, and outdoor spaces that become the places people never want to leave.

**Why it works:** Opens with commitment, describes experience, states quality, closes with outcome.

### Tagline System

| Type | Tagline | Usage |
|------|---------|-------|
| Primary | "Linger Longer" | Universal — all audiences, all channels |
| Emotional | "Your Shade Sanctuary" | Residential, hospitality, lifestyle |
| Authority | "Luxury Shade" | Brand descriptor, positioning, trade |
| Seasonal | "Luxury Summer" | Campaigns, seasonal pushes |

### Voice Equation
> *Harbour Outdoor sophistication + approachable expertise = ShadeScapes*

Positioning: Not Gucci (inaccessible). Not Wayfair (commodity). The refined middle — warm, knowledgeable, confident.

### Core Voice Attributes

| Attribute | In Practice |
|-----------|-------------|
| Confident | Authority earned through 24 years of expertise |
| Knowledgeable | Educates rather than sells |
| Approachable | Sophisticated does not mean cold |
| Passionate | Genuine enthusiasm for elegant shade design |
| Refined | Polished without being pretentious |

### Vocabulary Rules

| Never Say | Say Instead |
|-----------|-------------|
| Yard | Outdoor space |
| Backyard | Outdoor living area, outdoor retreat |
| Patio | Outdoor room, living space |
| Products | Solutions, designs, collection, portfolio |
| Customers | Clients, partners |
| Sales team | Shade experts |
| Buy now | Begin your project |
| Cheap / Affordable | Investment, value |

### Five Pillars of Positioning
1. **Curator, Not Retailer** — Select, don't stock everything
2. **Partner, Not Vendor** — Collaborative, invested in outcomes
3. **Value, Not Price** — Never compete on cost; educate on investment
4. **Experience, Not Product** — Create environments, not transactions
5. **Heritage, Not Hype** — Legacy and craftsmanship over marketing claims

### Avatar Example: Luxury Residential

**Emotional Drivers:** The gathering place, resort feeling at home, personal style expression, timeless investment, the "finished" feeling.

**Headlines & Hooks:**
- "Create the gathering place"
- "Resort living. No reservation required."
- "Where weekends feel like vacations"
- "Built for the moments that matter"
- "An investment in how you live"
- "Crafted to outlast trends"

**Pain Points Addressed:**

| Pain Point | Solution Phrase |
|------------|----------------|
| "Outdoor space looks unfinished" | "The finishing touch your space deserves" |
| "Umbrellas look cheap" | "Shade that matches your style" |
| "Want to entertain more" | "Create the gathering place" |
| "Everything fades or breaks" | "Crafted to outlast trends" |

**CTAs:** "Explore our collection" / "Schedule a consultation" / "See what's possible" / "Begin your outdoor transformation"

### Avatar Example: Design Professionals

**Core Positioning:** Teaching-first. 60% value, 20% entertainment, 20% other. Insider knowledge.

**Emotional Drivers:** Peer-level respect, time savings, client protection, honest expertise.

**Value-Forward Teaching Phrases:**
- "Why European-influenced designers still lead in wind load engineering"
- "The spec mistake that delays 90% of outdoor projects"
- "Wind ratings explained — without the jargon"

**Wind Truth (Critical Differentiator):**
- "No umbrella is windproof. Here's what actually matters."
- "Wind-resistant ≠ wind-proof. Know the difference."

## 3. Quality Checks by Phase

### Mission Statement
- [ ] Contains action verb (not "is" or "provides")
- [ ] Internal-facing (guides decisions, not marketing copy)
- [ ] Timeless (no dates, campaigns, or trends)
- [ ] Specific enough to differentiate from competitors
- [ ] Client approved

### Brand Promise
- [ ] Customer-facing (about their experience)
- [ ] Specific enough to differentiate
- [ ] Deliverable every single time
- [ ] Something they could be held accountable to
- [ ] Client approved

### Taglines
- [ ] Primary works for ALL audiences
- [ ] Each serves a different purpose
- [ ] Reinforces brand positioning (not generic)
- [ ] Memorable and distinct from competitors
- [ ] Client approved

### Voice & Tone
- [ ] Voice equation creates a unique position
- [ ] Attributes have practical meaning (not vague)
- [ ] Word banks include 10+ words per category
- [ ] Clear distinction from competitor voice
- [ ] Client approved

### Vocabulary Rules
- [ ] Addresses industry commodity language
- [ ] "Say Instead" options reinforce positioning
- [ ] Includes client-specific preferences
- [ ] Practical and enforceable

### Avatar Phrase Banks
- [ ] Based on research, not assumptions
- [ ] Headlines are specific (not generic marketing)
- [ ] Pain points match real customer language
- [ ] CTAs are action-oriented and on-brand
- [ ] Client reviewed per avatar before moving to next
